What stood out

Five of six talked about keeping their effort going. In Q3, P1 said "willpower," P2 "busy school works," P3 "consistency," P5 "some days I’m unmotivated," and P6 "nobody keeps me accountable, motivation issues." Three also didn't know what to do next: P1 ("lack of expert guidance"), P5 ("learn more specifics"), and P6 ("not sure where to start"). I can't really tell what P4 meant by "the greed" from two words, and P4 didn't want a follow-up.

Most people had used a tool, but few said how it went. Five of six said yes to Q4. Q4 also asked "What happened?", but only P2 ("worked efficently") and P6 ("couldn't keep up") said how it went. P6 also guessed why: "maybe because my plan was not strategic enough." The others just named the tool. I couldn't ask follow-ups in a survey, so I added this to the interview guide (D3, Q4).

The two people who said no to Q5 still had trouble with their goals. Q5 asked if a tool that breaks a goal into small steps and checks in would help. P1, P2, P4, and P6 said yes. P3 said no because they don't use apps and one didn't help before. P5 said no because "I like to plan my own things out," but in Q3 still said some days they're unmotivated. With only six responses I'm not calling this a pattern. I just took it as something to ask about in the interviews.

Interview guide

Text in [brackets] comes from that person's screener answers. I asked follow-ups one at a time, after they answered the main question. I only asked probes if the answer didn't already cover them. Before Q5, I described the tool the same way the screener did: "a tool that breaks a big goal into small steps and checks in with you."

  1. In the screener, you described a goal: [Q2 answer]. Can you tell me about it?
    Probe: Why did you choose that goal?
  2. What, if anything, have you done toward that goal since you filled out the screener?
    Probe: How, if at all, has that changed over time? If they've done nothing: Why not?
  3. You said [Q3 answer] made it hard to follow through. Can you tell me about a time that happened?
    Probe: Why do you think that happens?
  4. You mentioned [Q4 answer]. How did you use it?
    Follow-ups: Are you still using it? Why or why not? If they used no tool: How do you keep track of the goal, if at all?
  5. How, if at all, would you want a tool like this to work for you?
    Follow-up: Here are a few ways it could work.
    • A: You set the steps and schedule; the tool only reminds you.
    • B: The tool suggests steps and a schedule; you approve or change them.
    • C: The tool sets the steps and schedule; you follow them.
    • D: None of these.
    Which, if any, is closest to what you would want? Why?
  6. Do you think you would use a tool like this? Why or why not?
    Follow-up, if yes: Do you think you would continue to use it? Why or why not?
  7. Is there anything about working toward this goal that I didn't ask about and you think matters?

Q5 and Q6 ask about a tool that doesn't exist yet. So I count those answers as weaker evidence than Q2 to Q4, which are about what people actually did.

Full interviews

Each question is written the way I asked it, with the brackets filled in from that person's screener. I only listed the probes and follow-ups I actually asked. My own notes are separate, at the end of each interview. Highlighted parts are the ones I used in the synthesis (D4).

P1

Oct 12, 2026 · in person · 30 min · consented to notes

Q1 · In the screener, you described a goal: learning a skill. Can you tell me about it?

"I've been trying to learn shader programming for WebGL. It's kind of a mix of math and front-end stuff, and I wanted to get it for my portfolio."

Q1 probe · Why did you choose that goal?

"Honestly it's hard to stand out if you only do normal UI. Real-time graphics on websites seemed like the next big thing, so I wanted to learn it."

Q2 · What, if anything, have you done toward that goal since you filled out the screener?

"I did two tutorials on fragment shaders and took some notes in Obsidian."

Q2 probe · How, if at all, has that changed over time?

"The beginning was fine, I made basic shapes. But once the vector math got into 3D lighting, I got really slow."

Q3 · You said a lack of expert guidance to follow, and willpower, made it hard to follow through. Can you tell me about a time that happened?

"Last Tuesday I had a bug and the canvas just stayed blank. When you're teaching yourself, you can't tell if it's the code or if your math is wrong. I stared at it for like two hours, and the next day I didn't even want to open it."

Q3 probe · Why do you think that happens?

"Because there's nobody to tell you 'you're close, check line 12.' When I get stuck and nobody helps, I just start avoiding it."

Q4 · You mentioned using ChatGPT to plan in detail how to execute on a day-to-day basis. How did you use it?

"I asked it to make a 4-week plan from beginner GLSL to lighting, with one exercise every day."

Q4 follow-up · Are you still using it?

"Kind of. Now I mostly use it when I have a concept question."

Q4 follow-up · Why or why not?

"The plan was too strict. Stuff comes up, and once I was two days behind, the schedule didn't make sense anymore."

Q5 · How, if at all, would you want a tool like this to work for you?

"I'd want it to be like a senior who knows the stuff. It tells me the next small problem to work on and helps when I'm stuck, but I'm still the one doing it."

Q5 follow-up · Which of A, B, C, or D, if any, is closest to what you would want? Why?

"B. I want it to plan the path since it knows more than me, but I want to decide the schedule, because some days I just don't have the energy."

Q6 · Do you think you would use a tool like this? Why or why not?

"Yeah, if the suggestions actually make sense, and if it changes the plan when I fall behind instead of making me feel guilty."

Q6 follow-up · Do you think you would continue to use it? Why or why not?

"Only if it remembers where I was. If every day feels like starting over and it doesn't know where I got stuck, I'd stop using it."

Q7 · Is there anything about working toward this goal that I didn't ask about and you think matters?

"How fast you go matters more than reminders. If it keeps sending notifications while I'm focused, it ruins it."

My notes after the interview (my take, not P1's words). What surprised me: P1 had plenty of drive. Their motivation dropped once they got stuck on the math and had no one to ask. Compared with the screener: This matches what P1 wrote. ChatGPT was good for planning out a syllabus, but it couldn't help when something broke. Follow-up I should have asked: "When ChatGPT generated your daily schedule, what did you do on the very first day you missed a task?"

P2

Oct 16, 2026 · in person · 30 min · consented to notes

Q1 · In the screener, you described a goal: working on various projects. Can you tell me about it?

"I have a couple studio projects, a slide deck for a team case competition, and my personal website that I want to clean up."

Q1 probe · Why did you choose that goal?

"It's the middle of the semester, so if I don't keep up with all of them, my grades drop and I miss portfolio deadlines."

Q2 · What, if anything, have you done toward that goal since you filled out the screener?

"I finished my studio wireframes and made three slides for the competition."

Q2 probe · How, if at all, has that changed over time?

"I kind of gave up on the website. Studio and the team project have real deadlines, so my own stuff got pushed back."

Q3 · You said being busy with schoolwork made it hard to follow through. Can you tell me about a time that happened?

"Last Thursday I planned two hours for my site. But my team had to redo stuff for a critique and we worked until 11 PM. I was so tired I just went to bed."

Q3 probe · Why do you think that happens?

"School deadlines actually matter because professors grade you. My own projects only matter to me, so they always lose."

Q4 · You mentioned you had used an app, planner, or AI tool, and it worked efficiently. Which tool was it, and how did you use it?

"Todoist with Google Calendar. I just throw tasks into 30-minute blocks really fast."

Q4 follow-up · Are you still using it?

"Yeah, every day."

Q4 follow-up · Why or why not?

"Because adding a task takes like two seconds. If an app takes five minutes to set up, I won't use it."

Q5 · How, if at all, would you want a tool like this to work for you?

"It could look at my calendar, find the free time between classes, and say something like 'you have 45 minutes, just do this one Figma frame.'"

Q5 follow-up · Which of A, B, C, or D, if any, is closest to what you would want? Why?

"A. I know my school work better than an app does. I don't want AI planning my day. I just want a reminder of the small thing when I have a free hour."

Q6 · Do you think you would use a tool like this? Why or why not?

"Only if it works with the apps I already use. If it's a new app I have to open and keep up with, probably not."

Q6 follow-up · Do you think you would continue to use it? Why or why not?

"If it makes things easier to think about, yes. But if it sends notifications when I'm already stressed in class, I'd mute it in like three days."

Q7 · Is there anything about working toward this goal that I didn't ask about and you think matters?

"Energy matters more than time. 30 free minutes at 9 PM is not the same as 30 minutes at 11 AM."

My notes after the interview (my take, not P2's words). What surprised me: P2 really wants to stay in control. They don't want complicated features or an AI planning their day. Compared with the screener: This matches "busy school works," since school deadlines push out personal projects. The tool they called "efficient" turned out to be simple time blocking. Follow-up I should have asked: "What is the trigger that actually makes you check off a task on a busy day?"

P6

Oct 17, 2026 · in person · 30 min · consented to notes

Q1 · In the screener, you described a few goals: learning new UX tools for your design process, time management, job searching, and using AI tools to keep up with the trend. Can you tell me about them?

"I'm trying to get into product design, so I feel like I need to learn Figma variables, try the new AI UI tools, fix my resume, and network on LinkedIn, all at the same time."

Q1 probe · Why did you choose those goals?

"The job market is really bad right now. Every job post wants someone who can do everything, and I feel behind."

Q2 · What, if anything, have you done toward those goals since you filled out the screener?

"I saved like fifteen tutorials and made a huge Notion board of jobs to apply to."

Q2 probe · How, if at all, has that changed over time?

"I haven't actually applied anywhere or finished a tutorial. Saving stuff feels like I'm working, but then I look at the list and I just freeze."

Q3 · You said nobody keeping you accountable, motivation, keeping track, and not being sure where to start made it hard to follow through. Can you tell me about a time that happened?

"Last Sunday I sat down at 10 AM to work on a case study. I organized my Notion for two hours, then spent an hour deciding if I should learn Framer or Figma first. I got overwhelmed and ended up on social media."

Q3 probe · Why do you think that happens?

"When you have four big goals and no boss or teacher checking, you don't know what to do first. There are too many options so you freeze."

Q4 · You mentioned using the Apple Notes app and a physical planner, but you couldn't keep up. How did you use them?

"Every Monday I'd write a big 10-item to-do list in my notebook, or just dump links into Notes."

Q4 follow-up · Are you still using them?

"Not really. I stop looking at the page after two days."

Q4 follow-up · Why or why not?

"When it's Wednesday and seven things aren't crossed off, it feels like I failed, so I just stop looking at the planner."

Q5 · How, if at all, would you want a tool like this to work for you?

"I want it to tell me, 'Don't worry about the other ten things today, just open Figma and do this one 15-minute component.' And then check on me at 4 PM to see if I did it."

Q5 follow-up · Which of A, B, C, or D, if any, is closest to what you would want? Why?

"B. I need something to make the first move and cut it down for me, but I still want to be able to say 'I can't do that today.'"

Q6 · Do you think you would use a tool like this? Why or why not?

"Yes, definitely. I have the motivation, I just freeze up."

Q6 follow-up · Do you think you would continue to use it? Why or why not?

"Only if it's chill when I miss things. If it makes me feel guilty or the missed tasks pile up into a huge list, I'll quit it like I did with my planner."

Q7 · Is there anything about working toward these goals that I didn't ask about and you think matters?

"Starting is the hard part, emotionally. When you're job hunting every task feels heavy, so making it easier to start is huge."

My notes after the interview (my take, not P6's words). What surprised me: Organizing was basically a way for P6 to put off the real work, because actually making something felt harder. Compared with the screener: This matches "not strategic enough" and the accountability problem. The planner stopped working because the lists kept getting too big. Follow-up I should have asked: "Would an AI check-in feel like real accountability to you, or does accountability have to come from another human?"

What changed from the first guide

First guideRevisedWhy
"Walk me through the last time you tried to work toward that goal. What did a normal day/week actually look like?"Q1, Q2A goal and a "normal day" may not be connected. The revised questions ask about the goal and what the person has done, without assuming a routine.
"When did you first notice yourself losing momentum? What was happening around then?"Q2 probeAssumed the person lost momentum, and that they understand "momentum" the way I do. The probe asks whether their effort changed, in either direction.
"You said you [used / didn't use] a tool for this. Walk me through what that actually looked like."Q4Adds "Why or why not?" so the interview gets at why a tool was kept or dropped, which the screener mostly didn't answer.
"What would have to be true for you to trust a tool with something this personal?"RemovedAssumed the goal is personal and that trust is the issue. Trust can still come up in the "why" answers to Q5 and Q6 without my raising it.
"In the ideal version of this, who's in control day to day: you, or the tool?"Q5"Control" wasn't defined and the question offered only two extremes. Q5 first asks openly how they'd want it to work, then describes three specific levels plus "none of these."
"What would make you stop using something like this after a week?"Q6Assumed the person would stop, and the one-week time frame had no basis. Q6 asks whether they would use it at all, then whether they would continue, and why.

Where participants disagreed

How much should the tool plan?

  • P1 (picked B): wants it "to plan the path since it knows more than me."
  • P6 (picked B): wants "something to make the first move and cut it down for me."
  • P2 (picked A): only wants reminders. "I don't want AI planning my day."

P2 is also the only one whose current tool still works every day (Q4 follow-up), so maybe a planning tool matters less for P2.

P2's answers don't fully match.

  • P2 picked A, where the tool only reminds you (Q5 follow-up).
  • But when I asked openly how it should work, P2 said it could "look at my calendar, find the free time between classes" and suggest one small task (Q5). That's closer to B, because the tool is choosing when and what.

So I think P2 doesn't mind suggestions, as long as they're small and fit into time P2 already has. P2 seems to push back on the word "planning" more than on the tool actually helping. I didn't catch this during the interview, so it's something to ask about in usability testing (D7).

Check-ins.

  • P6 asked for a check-in (Q5).
  • P1 and P2 both said constant notifications would make them stop (Q7; Q6 follow-up).

Maybe one check-in at a time the person picks could work for both, but I didn't test that.

Limits

  • Three interviews is a small sample.
  • All three said yes to the tool question in the screener (Q5), so I didn't hear from the people who doubted a tool the most. P3 ("I don't use apps for anything") and P5 ("I like to plan my own things out") weren't interviewed.
  • Q5 and Q6 ask about a tool that doesn't exist, so those answers are weaker than what people said they actually did in Q2 to Q4.
  • All three are students or job seekers in design or computing.

What this means for D5

  1. The tool suggests steps, and the person can approve, change, or skip them. The tool never sets the plan by itself (no one picked C in Q5).
  2. The tool shows one next step at a time, sized to the time available (P2 and P6, Q5).
  3. After a missed day, the tool re-plans instead of piling up overdue tasks, and it doesn't make the person feel guilty (P1, Q6; P6, Q4 and Q6 follow-up).
  4. The tool remembers where the person got stuck and picks up from there (P1, Q6 follow-up).
  5. The tool only checks in a few times, at a time the person picks (P1, Q7; P2, Q6 follow-up; P6, Q5).
  6. The tool connects to a calendar people already use, so they don't have to manage a new app (P2, Q6 and Q4 follow-up).
