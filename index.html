What stood out

Five of six named something about keeping effort going. In Q3, P1 said "willpower," P2 "busy school works," P3 "consistency," P5 "some days I’m unmotivated," and P6 "nobody keeps me accountable, motivation issues." Three also said they didn't know what to do next: P1 ("lack of expert guidance"), P5 ("learn more specifics"), and P6 ("not sure where to start"). I can't interpret P4's "the greed" from two words, and P4 declined a follow-up.

Most people had used a tool, but few said how it went. Five of six answered yes to Q4. Q4 asked "What happened?", but only P2 ("worked efficently") and P6 ("couldn't keep up") described an outcome. P6 also gave a guess at why: "maybe because my plan was not strategic enough." The other answers name the tool and stop. A survey can't ask a follow-up, so this went into the interview guide (D3, Q5).

The two "no" answers to Q5 were about the tool, not the goal. Q5 asked whether a tool that breaks a goal into small steps and checks in would help. P1, P2, P4, and P6 said yes. P3 said no because they don't use apps and one didn't help before. P5 said no because "I like to plan my own things out," but still reported days without motivation in Q3. With six responses, I'm treating this as something to ask about in interviews, not as a pattern.

D3 · Interviews

Revised interview guide

Revised Oct 5, 2026 · interviews in progress

I rewrote the interview guide to remove leading questions and to cover the gaps above. I'm redoing the interviews with this guide. Full responses for each participant will be posted here, numbered the same way as the screener.

Interview guide

Text in [brackets] is filled in from that participant's screener answers. Probes are asked only if the first answer doesn't already cover them. Before Q6, I describe the tool the same way the screener did: "a tool that breaks a big goal into small steps and checks in with you." Q6 to Q8 refer to that tool.

  1. In the screener, you described a goal: [Q2 answer]. Can you tell me about it? Probe: Why did you choose that goal?
  2. Have you worked on that goal since you filled out the screener? If so, what have you done? Probe: How often do you work on it?
  3. Has the time or effort you put into it changed since you set it? If so, how? Probe: What else was going on at that time?
  4. You said [Q3 answer] made it hard to follow through. Can you give me an example? Probe: Why do you think that happens?
  5. You mentioned [Q4 answer]. How did you use it? Are you still using it? Why or why not? If they used no tool: How do you keep track of the goal, if at all?
  6. Here are a few ways a tool like this could work. A: You set the steps and schedule; the tool only reminds you. B: The tool suggests steps and a schedule; you approve or change them. C: The tool sets the steps and schedule; you follow them. D: None of these. Which, if any, is closest to what you would want? Why?
  7. If you used a tool like this, what information about your goal and your schedule, if any, would you be willing to share with it? Is there anything you would not share? Why?
  8. Do you think you would use a tool like this? If so, do you think you would continue to use it? Why or why not?
  9. Is there anything about working toward this goal that I didn't ask about and you think matters?

What changed from the first guide

First guideRevisedWhy
"Walk me through the last time you tried to work toward that goal. What did a normal day/week actually look like?"Q1, Q2A goal and a "normal day" may not be connected. The revised questions ask about the goal and what the person has done, without assuming a routine.
"When did you first notice yourself losing momentum? What was happening around then?"Q3Assumed the person lost momentum, and that they understand "momentum" the way I do. Q3 asks whether effort changed, in either direction.
"You said you [used / didn't use] a tool for this. Walk me through what that actually looked like."Q5Adds "Why or why not?" so the interview gets at why a tool was kept or dropped, which the screener mostly didn't answer.
"What would have to be true for you to trust a tool with something this personal?"Q7Assumed the goal is personal and that trust is the issue. Q7 asks what, if anything, the person would share, and leaves room for "nothing."
"In the ideal version of this, who's in control day to day: you, or the tool?"Q6"Control" wasn't defined and the question offered only two extremes. Q6 describes three specific levels, plus "none of these" so the person isn't pushed into wanting a tool.
"What would make you stop using something like this after a week?"Q8Assumed the person would stop, and the one-week time frame had no basis. Q8 first asks whether they would use it at all, then whether they would continue, and why.
