What stood out

Five of six named something about keeping effort going. In Q3, P1 said "willpower," P2 "busy school works," P3 "consistency," P5 "some days I’m unmotivated," and P6 "nobody keeps me accountable, motivation issues." Three also said they didn't know what to do next: P1 ("lack of expert guidance"), P5 ("learn more specifics"), and P6 ("not sure where to start"). I can't interpret P4's "the greed" from two words, and P4 declined a follow-up.

Most people had used a tool, but few said how it went. Five of six answered yes to Q4. Q4 asked "What happened?", but only P2 ("worked efficently") and P6 ("couldn't keep up") described an outcome. P6 also gave a guess at why: "maybe because my plan was not strategic enough." The other answers name the tool and stop. A survey can't ask a follow-up, so this went into the interview guide (D3, Q5).

The two "no" answers to Q5 were about the tool, not the goal. Q5 asked whether a tool that breaks a goal into small steps and checks in would help. P1, P2, P4, and P6 said yes. P3 said no because they don't use apps and one didn't help before. P5 said no because "I like to plan my own things out," but still reported days without motivation in Q3. With six responses, I'm treating this as something to ask about in interviews, not as a pattern.

Interview guide

Text in [brackets] is filled in from that participant's screener answers. Follow-ups are asked one at a time, after the main question is answered. Probes are asked only if the answer doesn't already cover them. Before Q5, I describe the tool the same way the screener did: "a tool that breaks a big goal into small steps and checks in with you."

  1. In the screener, you described a goal: [Q2 answer]. Can you tell me about it? Probe: Why did you choose that goal?
  2. What, if anything, have you done toward that goal since you filled out the screener? Probe: How, if at all, has that changed over time? If they've done nothing: Why not?
  3. You said [Q3 answer] made it hard to follow through. Can you tell me about a time that happened? Probe: Why do you think that happens?
  4. You mentioned [Q4 answer]. How did you use it? Follow-ups: Are you still using it? Why or why not? If they used no tool: How do you keep track of the goal, if at all?
  5. How, if at all, would you want a tool like this to work for you? Follow-up: Here are a few ways it could work. A: You set the steps and schedule; the tool only reminds you. B: The tool suggests steps and a schedule; you approve or change them. C: The tool sets the steps and schedule; you follow them. D: None of these. Which, if any, is closest to what you would want? Why?
  6. Do you think you would use a tool like this? Why or why not? Follow-up, if yes: Do you think you would continue to use it? Why or why not?
  7. Is there anything about working toward this goal that I didn't ask about and you think matters?

Q5 and Q6 ask about a tool that doesn't exist yet, so I treat those answers as weaker evidence than Q2 to Q4, which ask what people have actually done.

Full interviews

P1

Oct 17, 2026 · in person · 30 min · consented to notes

Q1 · In the screener, you described a goal: learning a skill. Can you tell me about it?

"I’ve been trying to learn shader programming for WebGL. It’s a mix of math and front-end design that I’ve wanted to grasp for portfolio work."

Q1 probe · Why did you choose that goal?

"It’s hard to stand out just doing standard UI. Real-time graphics feel like the next frontier for interactive web experiences."

Q2 · What, if anything, have you done toward that goal since you filled out the screener?

"I worked through two tutorials on fragment shaders and took some notes in Obsidian."

Q2 probe · How, if at all, has that changed over time?

"I started strong with basic shapes, but as soon as the vector math got into 3D lighting models, my pace dropped off sharply."

Q3 · You said a lack of expert guidance to follow, and willpower, made it hard to follow through. Can you tell me about a time that happened?

"Last Tuesday I hit a math bug where the canvas stayed blank. When you're self-taught, you don't know if your code syntax is wrong or your linear algebra logic is broken. Staring at it for two hours just completely drained my motivation to open it the next day."

Q3 probe · Why do you think that happens?

"When the feedback loop breaks and there's no mentor to say ‘you’re almost there, check line 12,’ friction turns into avoidance."

Q4 · You mentioned using ChatGPT to plan in detail how to execute on a day-to-day basis. How did you use it?

"I asked it to break down a 4-week curriculum from beginner GLSL to lighting models, giving me one prompt or exercise per day."

Q4 follow-up · Are you still using it?

"On and off. Mostly just when I need to ask conceptual questions."

Q4 follow-up · Why or why not?

"The daily plan it made was too rigid. Real life happens, and once I fell two days behind, the schedule stopped making sense."

Q5 · How, if at all, would you want a tool like this to work for you?

"I’d want it to behave like a tech lead, someone who can scope the milestone, give me the right bite-sized problem, and debug my conceptual block without taking over the driving."

Q5 follow-up · Which of A, B, C, or D, if any, is closest to what you would want? Why?

"B. I want its domain knowledge to lay out the path, but I need final control over my schedule based on my actual mental energy that day."

Q6 · Do you think you would use a tool like this? Why or why not?

"Yes, if the suggestions are actually technically sound and adapt when I fall behind instead of just piling on guilt."

Q6 follow-up · Do you think you would continue to use it? Why or why not?

"Only if it provides continuous context. If it treats every day like day one and doesn't remember where I got stuck, I’ll drop it."

Q7 · Is there anything about working toward this goal that I didn't ask about and you think matters?

"Pacing matters more than reminders. When tools ping you constantly during deep work, it actually ruins your flow."

My notes after the interview (my interpretation, not the participant's words). What surprised me: They didn’t lack initiative; their willpower collapse was directly triggered by getting stuck on domain-specific ambiguity. Compared with the screener: Confirms the screener directly. ChatGPT was great for planning a syllabus, but couldn't replace active guidance when things broke. Follow-up I should have asked: "When ChatGPT generated your daily schedule, what did you do on the very first day you missed a task?"

P2

Oct 16, 2026 · in person · 30 min · consented to notes

Q1 · In the screener, you described a goal: working on various projects. Can you tell me about it?

"I have a few studio class deliverables, a team case competition slide deck, and a personal branding site I want to clean up."

Q1 probe · Why did you choose that goal?

"It’s mid-semester crunch time, and if I don’t juggle these right now, my grades slip and portfolio deadlines pass."

Q2 · What, if anything, have you done toward that goal since you filled out the screener?

"Finished the wireframes for studio and drafted three slides for the competition."

Q2 probe · How, if at all, has that changed over time?

"I dropped the personal website completely. Studio and team stuff have fixed deadlines, so solo work got shoved aside."

Q3 · You said being busy with schoolwork made it hard to follow through. Can you tell me about a time that happened?

"Last Thursday I planned to spend two hours on my site. But our team had an emergency critique rework that went until 11 PM. I was burnt out and just went straight to sleep."

Q3 probe · Why do you think that happens?

"Academic deadlines have real consequences with professors. Personal projects only answer to me, so they lose every time."

Q4 · You mentioned you had used an app, planner, or AI tool, and it worked efficiently. Which tool was it, and how did you use it?

"I used Todoist paired with Google Calendar. Just rapid dumping of tasks into 30-minute calendar blocks."

Q4 follow-up · Are you still using it?

"Yes, daily."

Q4 follow-up · Why or why not?

"It works because it takes two seconds to add a task. If an app takes five minutes of setup, I abandon it."

Q5 · How, if at all, would you want a tool like this to work for you?

"Look at my calendar, see the blank pockets between lectures, and suggest: ‘You have 45 minutes, just draft this one Figma frame.’"

Q5 follow-up · Which of A, B, C, or D, if any, is closest to what you would want? Why?

"A. I know my school workload better than any algorithm. I don't want an AI telling me how to organize my day; I just need it to remind me of the small step when I have a spare hour."

Q6 · Do you think you would use a tool like this? Why or why not?

"Only if it integrates with what I already use. If it’s a brand-new app I have to open and manage, honestly no."

Q6 follow-up · Do you think you would continue to use it? Why or why not?

"If it reduces cognitive load, yes. If it sends push notifications when I’m already stressed in class, I’ll mute it within three days."

Q7 · Is there anything about working toward this goal that I didn't ask about and you think matters?

"Energy levels matter more than time. A free 30-minute window at 9 PM isn't the same as 30 minutes at 11 AM."

My notes after the interview (my interpretation, not the participant's words). What surprised me: Highly protective of autonomy; allergic to complex features or prescriptive AI planning. Compared with the screener: Confirmed "busy school works" pushes out personal initiative; the tool they called "efficient" was actually just simple time-blocking. Follow-up I should have asked: "What is the trigger that actually makes you check off a task on a busy day?"

P6

Oct 16, 2026 · in person · 30 min · consented to notes

Q1 · In the screener, you described a few goals: learning new UX tools for your design process, time management, job searching, and using AI tools to keep up with the trend. Can you tell me about them?

"I’m trying to transition into product design, so I feel like I have to learn Figma advanced variables, test new generative UI tools, update my resume, and network on LinkedIn all at once."

Q1 probe · Why did you choose those goals?

"The job market is brutal right now. Every job listing wants someone who does everything, and I feel like I’m falling behind."

Q2 · What, if anything, have you done toward those goals since you filled out the screener?

"I bookmarked about fifteen tutorials and made a massive Notion board of jobs to apply for."

Q2 probe · How, if at all, has that changed over time?

"I haven't actually applied or finished a tutorial. Gathering resources feels like working, but then looking at the list makes me freeze up."

Q3 · You said nobody keeping you accountable, motivation, keeping track, and not being sure where to start made it hard to follow through. Can you tell me about a time that happened?

"Last Sunday, I sat down at 10 AM to work on a case study. I spent two hours organizing Notion boards, then an hour debating whether to learn Framer or Figma first, got overwhelmed, and ended up just scrolling social media."

Q3 probe · Why do you think that happens?

"When you have four huge goals with no manager or teacher checking your work, you don't know what priority one is. You're frozen by too many options."

Q4 · You mentioned using the Apple Notes app and a physical planner, but you couldn't keep up. How did you use them?

"I’d write down ambitious 10-item to-do lists in my notebook every Monday, or dump links into Notes."

Q4 follow-up · Are you still using them?

"Not consistently. I abandon the page after two days."

Q4 follow-up · Why or why not?

"Seeing seven uncrossed items on Wednesday feels like failure, so I just avoid looking at the planner completely."

Q5 · How, if at all, would you want a tool like this to work for you?

"I want it to tell me: ‘Don’t worry about the other ten things today. Just open Figma and do this one 15-minute component.’ And then check back at 4 PM to see if I actually clicked it."

Q5 follow-up · Which of A, B, C, or D, if any, is closest to what you would want? Why?

"B. I need someone or something else to make the first move and trim down the scope, but I still want the safety valve to say ‘I can't do that today.’"

Q6 · Do you think you would use a tool like this? Why or why not?

"Yes, desperately. The biggest issue isn't ambition, it's paralysis."

Q6 follow-up · Do you think you would continue to use it? Why or why not?

"Only if it handles failure gently. If it guilts me or lets missed tasks snowball into an impossible backlog, I’ll shut down like I did with my planner."

Q7 · Is there anything about working toward these goals that I didn't ask about and you think matters?

"The emotional side of starting. When you're job hunting, every task feels heavy. Lowering that activation energy is everything."

My notes after the interview (my interpretation, not the participant's words). What surprised me: "Organization" was actually acting as a procrastination coping mechanism to avoid the vulnerability of real output. Compared with the screener: Confirms the screener's admission of "not strategic enough" and accountability issues. Planner failure wasn't laziness, but scope creep. Follow-up I should have asked: "Would an AI check-in feel like real accountability to you, or does accountability have to come from another human?"

First guideRevisedWhy
"Walk me through the last time you tried to work toward that goal. What did a normal day/week actually look like?"Q1, Q2A goal and a "normal day" may not be connected. The revised questions ask about the goal and what the person has done, without assuming a routine.

"You said you [used / didn't use] a tool for this. Walk me through what that actually looked like."Q4Adds "Why or why not?" so the interview gets at why a tool was kept or dropped, which the screener mostly didn't answer.
"What would have to be true for you to trust a tool with something this personal?"RemovedAssumed the goal is personal and that trust is the issue. Trust can still come up in the "why" answers to Q5 and Q6 without my raising it.
"In the ideal version of this, who's in control day to day: you, or the tool?"Q5"Control" wasn't defined and the question offered only two extremes. Q5 first asks openly how they'd want it to work, then describes three specific levels plus "none of these."

Where participants disagreed

How much the tool should plan. P1 and P6 want it to propose the steps (Q5, B). P1 wants "its domain knowledge to lay out the path," and P6 wants "something else to make the first move and trim down the scope." P2 wants only reminders (Q5, A): "I don't want an AI telling me how to organize my day." P2 is also the only one with a tool that still works daily (Q4 follow-up), so a planning tool may matter less for P2.

Check-ins. P6 asked for a check-in (Q5). P1 and P2 both named constant notifications as a reason to stop (Q7; Q6 follow-up). One check-in at a time the person picks may cover both, but I didn't test that.

Limits

Three interviews is a small sample. All three said yes to the screener's tool question (Q5), so the people most skeptical of a tool aren't represented: P3 ("I don't use apps for anything") and P5 ("I like to plan my own things out") weren't interviewed. Q5 and Q6 ask about a tool that doesn't exist, so those answers are weaker than what people said they actually did in Q2 to Q4. All three participants are students or job seekers in design or computing fields.

What this means for D5

  1. The tool suggests steps; the person approves, changes, or skips them. No mode where the tool sets the plan alone (no one picked C in Q5).
  2. Show one next step at a time, sized to the time available (P2 and P6, Q5).
  3. After a missed day, re-plan instead of piling up overdue items, with no guilt language (P1, Q6; P6, Q4 and Q6 follow-up).
  4. Remember where the person got stuck and pick up from there (P1, Q6 follow-up).
  5. Few check-ins, at a time the person chooses (P1, Q7; P2, Q6 follow-up; P6, Q5).
  6. Connect to a calendar the person already uses rather than asking them to manage a new app (P2, Q6 and Q4 follow-up).

Open questions to carry into D5. Would an AI check-in feel like real accountability, or does it have to come from a person (from my P6 notes)? What do people do on the first day they miss a planned step (from my P1 notes)? Can the tool help with a subject-specific block like P1's, or only with scheduling?
